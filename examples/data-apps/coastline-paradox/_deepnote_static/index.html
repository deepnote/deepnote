Why the coast never ends
  1. 1
    Walk the coast with a stick

    Lay straight sticks end to end along the shore. Number of sticks × stick length = coastline.

  2. 2
    Shorter sticks find more coast

    A smaller stick reaches into every bay, cove and rock that a longer one skipped over, so the total grows.

  3. 3
    It never settles

    Coastlines are fractal: zoom in and there is always more wiggle. Shrink the stick to zero and the length goes to infinity.

L(ε) ≈ F · ε1 − D
ε is the stick length. D is the fractal dimension: 1 for a smooth curve, 2 for one that fills the plane.

Lengths down to each island’s data resolution come from walking OpenStreetMap coastlines. Below that, length is extrapolated using the island’s measured fractal dimension, and the lens shows illustrative fractal detail. Below atomic scale the physics stops applying.
