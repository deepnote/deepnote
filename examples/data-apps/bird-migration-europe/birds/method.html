Everything here comes from public data and fits in one notebook. The pipeline downloads the radar profiles, cleans them, keeps only night-time, adds wind, and reduces it all to the numbers on this page.

CLEANING (THRESHOLDS)

StepRule
Duplicate scansProfiles with the same radar, time and height (several scan tasks) are averaged.
Non-bird scatterdens set to 0 where sd_vvp < the radar's sd_vvp_threshold (2 m/s): uniform radial velocities indicate rain or insects, not birds.
Ground clutterBins whose centre is less than 200 m above the antenna are dropped, and so is everything above 4 km over the antenna.
RainA whole profile is dropped if ≥ 25% of its valid bins have total reflectivity dbz_all > 15 dBZ. This removed about 9% of profiles (median per radar).
Implausible densityA profile is dropped if any bin exceeds 1,000 birds/km³.
Empty / gap binsMissing dens inside the valid height range counts as 0 birds. Missing speeds are filled with that profile’s density-weighted mean speed.
Excluded radars{ex_n} Romanian radars ({excluded}) report densities 30–100× higher than every other network (99th-percentile VIR > 500 birds/km², vs. 1–45 elsewhere). That points to calibration or clutter, not birds, so they are excluded.
Low-sensitivity radars{n_low} radars almost never detect birds: their 90th-percentile nightly VIR is below 1 bird/km², even though their neighbours see plenty. This includes all German radars, whose BALTRAD profiles appear to filter out bird echoes. They are drawn as hollow rings on the map and left out of every count and statistic: {low}.
Night onlyProfiles between local sunset and next sunrise at each radar (computed with astral). A radar-night needs data in ≥ 60% of its hours to count.

METRICS

MetricDefinition
VIR, birds/km²Σ dens × Δh over valid bins: how many birds are above each km² of ground.
MTR, birds/km/hΣ dens × ff × Δh: birds crossing a 1 km line perpendicular to their flight. Nightly traffic = mean night-time MTR × night length.
Birds aloft (headline counter)Each 0.25° cell within {cover} km of a reporting radar takes that nearest radar’s hourly VIR, multiplied by the cell area and summed. This is a lower bound: it covers only the area the radars observe, and it undercounts birds below the lowest valid bin.
Relative trafficA radar-night’s traffic ÷ that radar’s median night. It removes calibration differences between radars, so radars can be pooled.
Tailwind, m/sWind vector · unit vector towards 225° (SW, the autumn migration direction). The direction is fixed, not the birds’ measured heading, so the wind measure doesn’t depend on the birds and the reasoning isn’t circular.
Airspeed|bird ground velocity − wind vector|, density-weighted per radar-night. Only radar-nights with mean VIR ≥ 2 birds/km² are used.

CAVEATS

Radar density depends on each radar’s calibration and on the assumed radar cross-section of 11 cm² per bird, so the absolute numbers are approximate. The pattern across nights is the robust part. The wind is IFS analysis, not ERA5 reanalysis; for 850–925 hPa winds the two agree closely. Insects can slip past the sd_vvp filter on warm September nights.
